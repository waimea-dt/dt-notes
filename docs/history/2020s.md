This decade's history is still developing. Large language models and other generative AI systems brought natural-language interaction and machine-generated content into everyday software. Cloud infrastructure is widely used by businesses, while 5G networks continue to expand unevenly between countries and communities.

<timeline>

- 2020: 4.6 Billion People Online

	The number of **Internet** users passed roughly **4.6 billion**, about **60%** of the world's population. Internet access had become the essential platform for **communication**, **work**, **entertainment**, **shopping** and **public services**.

	<captioned>

	![](./_assets/2020s/60-pc.png)

	60% of the world online

	</captioned>


- 2020: Apple Silicon

	Apple introduced the M1, a 5nm ARM-based system-on-chip for Macs. It combined CPU cores, GPU cores and shared memory in one package, delivering high performance with much lower power use than many desktop processors.

	<captioned>

	![](./_assets/2020s/apple-m1.jpg)

	Apple M1 system-on-chip

	</captioned>

- 2020: COVID-19 and Remote Computing

	The COVID-19 pandemic moved work, school, healthcare and social events online for many people. Video calls, cloud documents and online learning became essential, while unequal access to devices and reliable Internet exposed the **digital divide**.

	<captioned>

	![](./_assets/2020s/PLACEHOLDER.jpg)

	Computing during a global crisis

	</captioned>

- 2020: GPT-3

	OpenAI's GPT-3 showed how scaling a transformer language model to 175 billion parameters could produce fluent text, answer questions and generate code. It helped move large language models from research systems towards widely used software.

	<captioned>

	![](./_assets/2020s/gpt-3.jpg)

	GPT-3 language model

	</captioned>

- 2020: AlphaFold

	DeepMind's AlphaFold demonstrated that neural networks could predict the three-dimensional structure of proteins from their amino-acid sequences with remarkable accuracy. It showed how machine learning could contribute to scientific discovery, not only media and office software.

	<captioned>

	![](./_assets/2020s/alphafold.jpg)

	AlphaFold protein structure prediction

	</captioned>

- 2022: ChatGPT

	ChatGPT made conversational access to a large language model available to the general public. It was fine-tuned with human feedback so it could follow instructions more naturally, and its rapid adoption changed how people thought about search, writing and software assistants.

	<captioned>

	![](./_assets/2020s/chatgpt.jpg)

	ChatGPT interface

	</captioned>

- 2022: Diffusion image generation

	Diffusion models made high-quality image generation from text prompts widely accessible. They gradually remove noise from random data to create an image, and their adoption expanded generative AI beyond text.

	<captioned>

	![](./_assets/2020s/diffusion-model.jpg)

	Diffusion image generation

	</captioned>


- 2025: 6 Billion People Online

	The number of **Internet** users passed roughly **6 billion**, about **75%** of the world's population.

	<captioned>

	![](./_assets/2020s/75-pc.png)

	75% of the world online

	</captioned>



- 2020s: Generative AI becomes mainstream

	Systems based on large neural networks began generating text, images, audio and video in response to prompts. They became common tools but also raised important questions about accuracy, copyright, privacy, employment and how people should evaluate machine-generated work.

	<captioned>

	![](./_assets/2020s/PLACEHOLDER.jpg)

	Generative AI in everyday software

	</captioned>

- 2020s: 5G networks expand

	Fifth-generation mobile networks increased wireless capacity and reduced latency in many locations. They support dense connected-device deployments as well as faster phones, with peak theoretical download speeds far above 4G.

	<captioned>

	![](./_assets/2020s/supercomputer.jpg)

	Modern supercomputer hardware

	</captioned>





</timeline>
