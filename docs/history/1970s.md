# 1970s - The Microchip Revolution

<timeline>

- 1970: Intel 1103 DRAM

	Intel introduced the 1103, an early commercially successful dynamic random-access memory chip. Memory could now be manufactured as an integrated circuit rather than assembled from larger components.

- 1971: Intel 4004 microprocessor

	Intel's 4004 put a central processing unit on one chip.

- 1971: Floppy disk

	IBM engineers led by Alan Shugart developed the floppy disk, making it easier to move software and data between computers.

- 1971: Email

	Networked messaging developed into email.

- 1971: Pascal

	Niklaus Wirth created Pascal as a language for teaching structured programming.

- 1972: C

	Dennis Ritchie and colleagues developed C at Bell Labs.

- 1972: Prolog

	Prolog introduced a logic-programming style.

- 1972: Pong

	Nolan Bushnell's Pong helped establish commercial video games and Atari.

- 1973: Ethernet

	Robert Metcalfe developed Ethernet for connecting computers and peripherals.

- 1973: Xerox Alto

	Xerox PARC's Alto combined Ethernet, a graphical interface and a mouse in a landmark personal workstation.

- 1974-1977: Personal computers appear

	Machines such as the Altair 8800, IBM 5100, TRS-80 and Commodore PET brought computing out of research institutions and into homes, clubs and small businesses.

- 1975: Microsoft begins

	After writing a BASIC interpreter for the Altair 8800, Paul Allen and Bill Gates formed Microsoft. Software became a major part of the emerging personal-computer industry.

- 1976: Apple I

	Steve Jobs and Steve Wozniak founded Apple and released the Apple I.

- 1977: Apple II

	The Apple II followed with colour graphics and cassette storage, helping make personal computing practical for hobbyists.

- 1979: WordStar

	MicroPro released WordStar, one of the first popular word processors. Word wrap, margins and printing made computers useful for everyday writing.

</timeline>
