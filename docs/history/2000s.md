# 2000s - The Mobile Computing Revolution

2000s INTRO HERE


<timeline>

- 2000: USB flash drives

	USB flash drives began replacing floppy disks as a convenient way to carry files. The first commercially available USB drive stored 8MB and cost $1000.

- 2000: C#

	Microsoft introduced C# as a modern language for the .NET platform. It was created as a direct competitor to Java, seeking to take some of its market share.

- 2001: Mac OS X

	Apple released Mac OS X with a Unix foundation and a new graphical interface. This new platform became the basis for all of apple's later OSes: MacOS, iPadOS, iPhoneOS

- 2001: iPod

	The iPod made it easy to carry a large music collection in a small device. The first iPod could store 1000 songs which was revolutionary at the time. Internally, the iPod used a tiny spinning hard drive.

- 2003: 64-bit CPUs

	64-bit processors became practical in consumer computers, allowing operating systems and applications to work with much larger memory spaces.

- 2004: Firefox

	Mozilla Firefox offered an open alternative in web browsing.

- 2004: Gmail

	Google launched Gmail with large storage and powerful search, showing how much software could be delivered as an online service.

- 2005: YouTube

	YouTube made publishing and watching user-created video simple through a web browser, accelerating the growth of online video and participatory media.

- 2007: iPhone

	Apple's iPhone combined a mobile phone, web browser and touch interface. Smartphones became general-purpose computers that people carried everywhere.


</timeline>
